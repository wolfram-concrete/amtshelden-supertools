# Editorial Radar

Editorial Radar treats strategy as a navigable field: structured enough to create trust, open enough to reveal what is still unknown. The visual system should feel like a command table for a careful editorial team, not like a pitch deck. Information is arranged in clear lanes, with source quality, mission, and public-facing value treated as equal citizens.

The composition uses warm paper, strong institutional green, quiet charcoal, and small signal accents. Large typographic gestures are reserved for the project state and the system logic; most text appears as compact labels, tags, and operational notes. The final artifact should look meticulously crafted, shaped through careful editorial judgment rather than decoration.

Space carries meaning: the Website, Discovery, Crawler, Quality, and Marketing pillars should read as separate operating zones but remain connected through thin lines and shared rhythm. Screenshots act as proof of current progress; matrices act as instruments for decision-making. Every block should be precise, restrained, and built with master-level attention.

The conceptual thread is public-sector curation: not “we crawl the internet,” but “we maintain a trusted observation system.” The design should make this shift visible. Raw signals flow inward, reviewed evidence flows outward, and only curated information reaches Behörden. The work must appear painfully deliberate, with every spacing, line, and label refined until it feels ready for a serious team conversation.
